## 🗺️ What it is

**Phygital Atlas** is a short interactive journey through seven places:

- a shore
- a trading floor
- a mountain glacier
- a river
- a city
- a cabin
- a ship at sea

Each place holds one small exercise that stands on its own. Along the way, students gather the evidence they need for the web page they make for the module.

> **"Phygital"** means physical, digital and AI, blended together. It is my picture of how work will feel in the near future, and the game lets students step into that world for a while.

## 💡 Why this game exists

Students receive a lot of information about their assignment: instructions, checklists, rubrics, what to do and when. All of it matters. But some things are hard to imagine until you start doing them, and often the hardest part is **the blank page**.

Phygital Atlas gives students a glimpse of what they will need to do. Like any game, it asks you to step in and work through a series of challenges. Here, each one prepares you for the kind of thinking and doing the assignment asks for, and at the end you see a visual example of what that work can become.

The game brings together what we do across the module: guest speakers from around the world talking about culture, different kinds of data, exercises that build teamwork, and the puzzles we solve together in class. It helps students connect those dots and take the first step.

## 🚫 What it is not

Phygital Atlas **does not replace** the classroom, the teaching, or the formal information students receive about their assignment. It is an example and a starting point, not a shortcut.

It is also an **experiment**. This is the very first version, and I want to see what works and what doesn't.

## 👀 A note for visitors

If you play Phygital Atlas without being on the module, it may not feel like a usual game. There is no score to beat and no level to win, and some places may not make full sense on their own. That is because it was made with a specific goal: to prepare students for their assignment.

It is one part of a bigger set of tools we use in the module, alongside classes, guest speakers, seminars and resources, and it is best seen that way rather than as a standalone game. If you teach, I hope it gives you ideas for your own classes.

## 🎧 How to play

- Open the link in any browser.
- There is nothing to install and no sign-in.
- It is best played **alone, with headphones**, at your own pace.

**There are two ways to play:**

- **[The full journey](https://irinagokhai-commits.github.io/phygital-atlas)** is the version my students play. You travel from place to place in order, from the beginning to the end.
- **[The game with jumps](https://irinagokhai-commits.github.io/phygital-atlas/places.html)** lets you jump between the different parts of the game. On the start page there is a button for each place, so you can go straight to the glacier, the river or any other place without playing the whole journey. You can also start without sound. This version is for visitors and colleagues who want to look around.

## 🌊 Sound

I paid particular attention to sound, because it is something I care about and I wanted the journey to feel more enjoyable. Each place has its own natural sounds: snow and ice underfoot, shifting sand, stones, water, rain and a crackling fire. They are there to make the game more immersive, and to add a creative touch of their own.
